## Titles

A title appears at the beginning of a page or section and provides a clear, high-level description of the content that follows.

### Guidelines

- Use titles to communicate the primary purpose of the page or section
- Keep titles concise and specific
- Avoid unnecessary words or filler
- Ensure titles are meaningful out of context (e.g., in navigation or search results)

### Capitalization

- Use sentence case for page titles

### Punctuation

- Do not use punctuation in titles
- Exception: Titles may include punctuation if they are written as a question

::: do

- *Payment settings*
- *Manage workspace access*
- *How do I update my billing information?*

:::

::: dont

- *Payment Settings.*
- *Manage your workspace access here*

:::

## Headings and Subheadings

Headings and subheadings organize content into scannable, logical sections, helping users quickly find relevant information.

### Guidelines

- Write headings that clearly describe the content that follows
- Keep headings concise and specific
- Use headings to break up dense or complex information
- Ensure headings are useful when scanned independently

::: do

- *Payment methods*
- *Update notification settings*

:::

::: dont

- *Information*
- *Details*

:::

### Hierarchy

Use headings in a clear, nested structure:

- H1: Page title
- H2: Primary sections
- H3+: Supporting sections

Do not skip heading levels (e.g., H2 → H4).

### Capitalization

Use sentence case for headings and subheadings (capitalize only the first word and proper nouns).

### Keywords and Clarity

- Include relevant keywords users are likely to search for
- Avoid vague or generic phrasing
- Focus on the main idea of the section

::: do

- *Error message guidelines*

:::

::: dont

- *Important information*

:::
