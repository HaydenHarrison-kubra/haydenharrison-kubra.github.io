Hyperlinks connect users to relevant internal or external resources.

## General guidelines

- Link meaningful, descriptive text, not generic phrases
- Ensure users understand where the link will take them
- Only link to trusted and relevant resources
- Always link legal document names, like Terms of Use, Terms and Conditions, and Privacy Policy (see [Legal Content](#legal-content/linking-legal-documents))

## Link text

- Write the sentence naturally, then link the most relevant words
- Avoid instructional phrases like "click here"

::: do

- *Read the brand guidelines for details (as a link on “brand guidelines”)*
- *View payment history*

:::

::: dont

- *Click here to read the brand guidelines*
- *Click here*

:::

## Articles and formatting

- Do not include preceding articles (a, an, the, our) in link text, unless the article is part of the name you're linking to
- Do not include punctuation unless it is part of the official title

::: do

- *Read brand guidelines*

:::

::: dont

- *Read the brand guidelines*

:::

## Tone and clarity

- Keep link text concise and specific
- Avoid vague or ambiguous wording

::: do

- *View account settings*

:::

::: dont

- *Learn more*

:::

## Accessibility considerations

- Link text should make sense out of context (e.g., for screen readers)
- Avoid using multiple identical link texts for different destinations

::: do

- *Download outage report*
- *View outage report history*

:::

::: dont

- *Click here (multiple times on a page)*

:::

## When in doubt

Link the fewest words that tell users where they're going.

::: details More detail

If you're unsure what to link:

1. Write the sentence first, without the link
1. Link the words that name the destination or action
1. Check that the link text makes sense on its own
1. Make sure no other link on the page uses the same text for a different destination

:::
